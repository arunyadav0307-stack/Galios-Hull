## 3. Frozen Present Convention

For a component field

\[
K_s=\mathbb F_{q^{m_s}}=\mathbb F_{p^{e m_s}},
\]

write `d_s=e m_s` and define

\[
\sigma(a)=a^{p^k},
\qquad
\rho(a)=a^{p^{d_s-k}}=\sigma^{-1}(a).
\]

The present code-first form and dual are

\[
\langle c,x\rangle_{s,k}=\sum_i c_i x_i^{p^k},
\qquad
C^{\perp_{k,\mathrm{cf}}}
 =\{x:\langle c,x\rangle_{s,k}=0\text{ for all }c\in C\}.
\]

Writing `C^{\perp_E}` for the ordinary Euclidean dual,

\[
C^{\perp_{k,\mathrm{cf}}}=\rho(C^{\perp_E}).
\]

The frozen principal reciprocal applies `rho` to coefficients, the principal twist is

\[
\lambda^{-p^{d_s-k}},
\]

and same-factor-set compatibility is

\[
\lambda^{1+p^{d_s-k}}=1.
\]

No Phase-4 evidence contradicts these definitions. The `p^k` reciprocal remains a convention comparator, not the frozen principal operation.

## 4. Source Convention

The directly accessible source is the arXiv HTML version of *Galois hulls of constacyclic codes over affine algebra rings*, arXiv:2412.08512v1, dated 11 December 2024. Its Section 2.2 displays

\[
\langle\boldsymbol\alpha,\boldsymbol\beta\rangle_k
 =\sum_i\alpha_i\beta_i^{p^k}
\]

and defines

\[
C^{\perp_k}
 =\{\boldsymbol\alpha:\langle\boldsymbol\alpha,\boldsymbol c\rangle_k=0
 \text{ for all }\boldsymbol c\in C\}.
\]

Thus the literal source convention places the **candidate** in the first slot and the codeword in the second slot. Frobenius is displayed on the second argument; in the dual equation it therefore acts on the codeword.

The same accessible section states in Lemma 1 that the dual twist is `lambda^{-p^{e-k}}`. Its Theorem 1 displays

\[
h^{\#}(x)=\sum_i h_0^{-p^{e-k}}h_i^{p^{e-k}}x^{l-i}
\]

and says that the dual is generated by `h#` and the hull by `lcm(g,h#)`. In the affine part, Theorem 4 repeats the `lambda^{-p^{e-k}}` dual twist, Theorem 5 repeats the `h_{S(\nu)}^{\#}` generator, Theorem 7 gives the lcm hull generator, and Theorem 8 gives a specialized hull-dimension formula.

The source's `e` is the exponent in its base field `q=p^e`. The present extension component requires `d_s=e m_s`; that extension-field exponent is not silently supplied by the source theorem.

## 6. Convention Equivalence Derivation

For any linear code `C`, the present code-first dual is

\[
D_{\mathrm{cf}}(C)
 =\{x:E(c,\sigma x)=0\ \forall c\in C\}
 =\sigma^{-1}(C^{\perp_E})
 =\rho(C^{\perp_E}).
\]

The literal source candidate-first dual is

\[
D_{\mathrm{cand}}(C)
 =\{a:E(a,\sigma c)=0\ \forall c\in C\}
 =(\sigma C)^{\perp_E}
 =\sigma(C^{\perp_E}).
\]

Therefore

\[
D_{\mathrm{cand}}(C)=\sigma^2(D_{\mathrm{cf}}(C)).
\]

The conventions are **not exactly identical**. They are equivalent as dual constructions only under the coordinatewise semilinear transformation `sigma^2`. They also become the same parameterized construction if the present code-first definition is reparameterized from `k` to the complementary iteration `d_s-k`; that is a different parameter, not the frozen present convention.

Equality of the two dual codes requires an additional invariance condition, such as `sigma^2(D_cf(C))=D_cf(C)`. Equality of the hulls for a fixed arbitrary code requires more: `C\cap D_cf(C)` need not equal `C\cap sigma^2(D_cf(C))`. Thus the source and present conventions are not interchangeable merely because both are called `k`-Galois duals.

The source's displayed inverse-Frobenius reciprocal and twist are exactly the formulas obtained from the present code-first convention, not from the literal candidate-first equations. This is the central literature-transfer obstruction.

## 7. Hull Equivalence Analysis

For a common simple-root factor set `F` and selected support `J`, the present code-first support is

\[
S_{\mathrm{cf}}=(F\setminus J)\cap\tau_\rho(J),
\]

while the literal candidate-first support is

\[
S_{\mathrm{cand}}=(F\setminus J)\cap\tau_\sigma(J).
\]

Since `tau_sigma=tau_rho^{-1}`, the supports can differ even when their weighted cardinalities agree. On a finite cycle, the number of directed `1\to0` transitions is equal to the number of directed `0\to1` transitions. Because Frobenius preserves factor degree around an orbit, the two orientations have the same weighted hull dimension for the tested compatible simple-root constacyclic selections. This is a special cyclic-orbit dimension fact, not equality of hull subspaces.

No general arbitrary-linear-code hull equality is claimed.

## 8. Reciprocal Equivalence Analysis

For a monic polynomial with nonzero constant term, define `#_rho` using the inverse-Frobenius coefficient action and `#_sigma` using the `p^k` coefficient action. Direct algebra gives

\[
(f^{\#_\rho})^{\#_\rho}=\rho^2(f),
\qquad
(f^{\#_\rho})^{\#_\sigma}=f.
\]

Thus `#_sigma` is the inverse operation to `#_rho`; `#_rho` is not generally an involution. The accessible source displays the rho-based operation, but the literal candidate-first dual equations select the sigma-based generator in the independent tests. Consequently:

- the displayed source reciprocal is equivalent to the present principal reciprocal only after converting candidate-first to code-first;
- it is not equivalent to the literal candidate-first dual for non-involutory parameters;
- the two factor permutations are inverse permutations on a common factor set;
- self-reciprocal/fixed-factor language must specify which reciprocal is meant.

## 9. Dual-Twist Equivalence Analysis

For a `lambda`-constacyclic code, the ordinary Euclidean dual has twist `lambda^{-1}`. Therefore

\[
D_{\mathrm{cf}}(C)=\rho(C^{\perp_E})
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{twist}(D_{\mathrm{cf}})=\lambda^{-p^{d_s-k}},
\]

whereas

\[
D_{\mathrm{cand}}(C)=\sigma(C^{\perp_E})
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{twist}(D_{\mathrm{cand}})=\lambda^{-p^k}.
\]

The source displays `lambda^{-p^{e-k}}` while displaying the candidate-first dual definition. This agrees with the frozen code-first formula in a base-field component (`d_s=e`) but not with the literal candidate-first derivation when `sigma` and `rho` differ.

In the independent F16-over-F4 nontrivial case `lambda=alpha^3`, `k=1`, `n=3`, the script reports candidate-first predicted twist `10` and code-first predicted twist `12` in the script's field representation. Both direct duals pass their own predicted constacyclic twist tests; the source's displayed rho candidate disagrees with the literal candidate-first dual for 6 of 8 selections.
